Trip planning: from orders to validated freight bookings

Current implementation snapshot: 31 October 2026. A standalone module next to loadmax/. It chooses solo/shared bookings and dates under capacity and arrival rules, with optional permissions, dated/weekly calendars, approved RDD overrides and named what-if runs. With a fleet cap, serving more shipments comes first; freight cost and bounded preferences come next. This describes implemented software scope, not approved operational policies.

The problem

Customers order cases for a required delivery date (RDD). The planner books trucks. Booking one truck per customer is simple but wasteful: trucks leave half empty and each trip pays a full rate.

  • Each order belongs to a ship-to (a delivery point, such as one store or distribution centre) and has an RDD (required delivery date), a hard deadline.
  • Each ship-to is served either as FTL (full truck load: the customer pays per truck trip) or LTL (less than truck load: the customer may pay per case instead of per truck).
  • Trucks come in types 4W, 6W, 10W, named by wheel count (4-wheel, 6-wheel, 10-wheel; an 18-wheel type, 18W, is priced on the rate card but has no capacity in the workbook). Capacity is two numbers, kilograms (kg) and cubic metres (m³), and both must hold. The 4W, 6W and 10W capacities come from the Type / KG / M3 table on the workbook's Pivot_Summary sheet, not from the VA05 order sheet.
  • Prices come from a rate card per ship-to: a trip rate per truck type, an optional handling charge (unloading), and for LTL customers a price per case.
  • Goal: cover eligible shipments with a calendar-open legal date exactly once, or report past-due/calendar-unavailable demand and fleet-cap leftovers. The capped objective minimizes leftover shipment count first, then freight plus bounded early-delivery/LTL-upgrade preferences. Excluded demand remains reconciled, not costed as delivered.

Planning and model terms

TermStands forMeaning here
MIPMixed-Integer ProgramAn optimization problem in which some decisions are whole numbers, here yes/no choices. A solver finds the lowest-cost answer that obeys every rule.
Set partitioning A MIP pattern: choose some pre-built groups so that every item is in exactly one chosen group. Here the items are shipments and the groups are candidate trips.
Candidate One possible trip that the optimizer may choose: either one shipment alone or a few that share a truck.
Solo A shipment sent on its own, with its cheapest trucks or its per-case price.
Rider An LTL shipment in a truck-priced booking with FTL. It has no separate case bill, but its destination can still increase the maximum trip rate or handling.
Baseline The cost if every shipment went alone, each at the cheaper of its truck and per-case price. The plan is judged against it.
Sheet rule How the workbook's Pivot_Summary sheet picks a truck (column EST Truck Usage): the smallest single truck that fits both kg and m³, otherwise several 6W. Every ship-to gets its own truck, with no sharing. Used as a second comparison point.
Dominance pruning Without a fleet cap, drop shared candidates that do not save against solos. With a cap, retain non-saving sharing and cost/truck alternatives because they may improve coverage.
Tie-break A bounded preference under 1 THB in total, such as fewer early days. It can outweigh a fractional-baht cost difference, not a full baht.
Penalty A cost bound making one fewer unplanned shipment preferable in a proven solve. A timeout/fallback may not achieve maximum coverage.
Gap (MIP gap) How far the solver's answer may be from the proven best possible answer. A gap of 0 means the answer is proven optimal.
Optimal Proven best objective over generated candidates and their supplied prices, not a proof over all possible routes, physical packings or commercial contracts.
Incumbent The best plan the solver has found so far, which is used if it runs out of time.
Component A group of shipments linked by shared candidates. Components do not affect each other, so each is solved on its own.
Utilization How full a truck is: load divided by capacity, by weight and by volume.
Reconciliation Checking that planned plus excluded quantities equal what was ordered.
HiGHS The open-source optimization solver used to solve the MIP, called through SciPy's milp function.

Validation is not exclusion

  • Workbook lines are checked before grouping: missing, malformed, nonfinite, boolean or negative cases/kg/m³ fail the build. Missing document/Ship-to/Sold-to/dates or conflicting Ship-to/Sold-to within one document/RDD also fail. An order retains its latest line document date.
  • Direct tables are checked too: required columns, valid dates, unique normalized order/customer/rate/truck keys and finite nonnegative quantities. Blank order/customer keys fail; a missing order ship-to is an eligibility exclusion. Optional missing prices are permitted, but negative/malformed/nonfinite monetary values fail.
  • Price semantics: blank/zero trip rate means not offered; blank handling means zero. MT case rate wins if both case rates exist. The workbook adapter converts zero case rates to blank; direct CSV zero case rates remain valid zero prices.
  • Publication: fatal validation or serialization failures do not replace a prior build/plan. Ordinary exclusions retain original quantities. Passing validation is not business approval.

Shipments and exclusions

A shipment is all orders for one ship-to with the same RDD (required delivery date). It is the unit that is planned: it is never split across trips (except by its solo fleet, below). Its release date is the latest order document date.

Pallets and truck loading

  • The planner checks two limits, kg and m³. In the August plan, of the 96 trips that run on a truck, 56 are held back more by weight and 40 more by volume. 24 are at least 90% full.
  • Freight averages 382 kg per m³. The 4W is built to 375 kg per m³ and the 10W to 429. The 6W is built to 200, which again points to its 25 m³ being too high.

Pricing rules

All pricing lives in pricing.py and baseline_cost.py. Nothing else computes a cost.

One shipment alone: the solo price

A shipment may need several trucks. The cheapest mix covering summed kg and m³ is found by integer enumeration (up to 2,000,000 combinations), otherwise a fleet MIP with a fixed 30-second limit and zero requested gap. Loads are divisible: this is not physical per-truck packing. Enumerated equal-cost mixes prefer fewer trucks, then smaller-ranked types. Nonoptimal fleet pricing is labelled, not silently called cheapest. For truck type $k$ and ship-to $j$:

\[ T_k(j) = \text{trip}_k(j) + \text{handling}_k(j) \]

A blank trip rate means that truck is not offered. Blank handling counts as 0.

  • FTL (full truck load) pays the fleet cost.
  • LTL (less than truck load) pays the cheaper of the fleet cost and the per-case bill: $\text{solo}(s)=\min\big(\text{case rate}\times\text{cases},\ \text{fleet cost}\big)$. The case rate is the MT (modern trade, large retail chains) rate if present, otherwise the GT (general trade, smaller shops) rate. A tie stays on the truck.

Several shipments on one truck

For one truck type $k$ carrying a set of drops $J$:

\[ \text{truck cost}(J,k) = \max_{j\in J} \text{trip}_k(j) + H, \qquad H=\begin{cases}\sum_{j} \text{handling}_k(j) & \text{per drop}\\ \max_j \text{handling}_k(j) & \text{per truck}\end{cases} \]
  • Every ship-to on the trip must offer $k$, and the combined kg and m³ must fit $k$.
  • If any shipment is FTL, use the truck cost with no additional LTL case bill. LTL destinations still participate in the maximum rate and handling, so adding a rider is not always free.
  • If all are LTL, the trip costs $\min\big(\sum_s \text{case rate}_s\times \text{cases}_s,\ \text{truck cost}\big)$.
  • Two shipments for the same ship-to count as one drop.
  • Optional permissions restrict all branches. Truck pricing requires every member to permit it, including LTL riders with FTL. Case pricing requires every member to be LTL, case-permitted and have a case rate. A missing case rate can still use the truck branch if all permit trucks; otherwise reject that shared price. Shared case billing still requires an offered physical truck type/capacity for all drops. Default cheaper-of-two and truck ties are unchanged.

ASSUMPTION: the highest trip rate is the multi-drop price, handling is per drop, and LTL is the cheaper of case and truck. These have not been checked against carrier invoices.

Candidate trips

The optimizer does not decide who may share a truck. candidates.py lists every acceptable trip first, then the model picks among them.

  • Solo candidates. Without a fleet cap, latest-date solos form a feasible baseline. With a cap, retain the nondominated cost/truck-count frontier, including mixed fleets and LTL case alternatives, over every legal departure date. The unconstrained baseline is not a constrained cost bound.
  • Shared trips of 2 up to max_shipments_per_trip (default 3) shipments on exactly one truck type.
  • Compatibility. Default same_province. Other modes: same_group, same_province_or_group, any, none. All member pairs must pass. Customer sharing denial and solo_only take precedence over pair overrides; for distinct ship-tos, deny/allow overrides precede the mode. Different shipments to the same ship-to may share even under none, unless sharing is denied. Province/group equality requires nonblank case-sensitive matching values. Drops count distinct ship-tos.
  • Dates. Intersect each member's departure window: from the later of release and RDD minus transit minus max_early_days, through RDD minus transit. Use the latest common date without a cap, all common dates with one. Each member must arrive no later than its RDD and no more than the early limit before it. Raw RDD spread is not a safe test when transit differs by province.
  • Calendar filtering. Filter the intersection by dispatch departure and each member's receiving arrival exceptions. Select latest surviving date without a cap; enumerate all surviving dates under a cap. An empty bounds window remains past due; a nonempty window fully blocked is calendar-unavailable. Individually available customers may have disjoint common dates, preventing sharing but preserving solos. No closure permits late or excessively early shipping. Resource frontiers retain only permitted case/truck alternatives.
  • Dominance pruning. Without a fleet cap, retain shared trips only when cheaper than solos. With a cap, retain non-saving consolidations too: fewer trucks can serve more shipments. The cheapest feasible single-truck type per set/date is retained because these alternatives all consume one truck.

The saved August default run has 1,512 candidates for 329 shipments. This is not a size guarantee: permissive sharing, larger candidate sets and cap-driven date expansion can grow rapidly.

The model

A set-partitioning MIP (mixed-integer program: an optimization problem with yes/no decisions), the same pattern as order_picking/chunker/chunk_model.py. Set partitioning means: choose candidate trips so that every shipment is in exactly one of them.

SymbolMeaning
$S$plannable shipments
$C$candidates (solo and shared). Candidate $c$ has members $M_c\subseteq S$, cost $a_c$, early days $e_c$, upgrade count $g_c$
$x_c\in\{0,1\}$candidate $c$ is used
$u_s\in\{0,1\}$shipment $s$ is left unplanned. Created for a shipment with no candidate (past due or calendar-unavailable), or for all shipments when a fleet cap is set
\[ \min \;\sum_{c\in C}\big(a_c + \varepsilon\, e_c + \varepsilon\, g_c\big)\,x_c \;+\; P\sum_{s\in S} u_s \] \[ \sum_{c:\, s\in M_c} x_c + u_s = 1 \quad \forall s\in S \qquad\text{(each shipment covered exactly once)} \] \[ \sum_{c:\,\text{date}(c)=d} \text{trucks}_c\, x_c \le N_{\max} \quad \forall d \qquad\text{(optional fleet cap)} \]

Here $N_{\max}$ is the most trucks allowed to leave on one date, $\text{trucks}_c$ is how many trucks candidate $c$ uses, and $\forall$ means "for every".

  • Penalty. Without caps, $P=\sum_s \text{solo}(s)+1$. With caps, use the sum of each member's maximum candidate cost plus 1. This safely covers costlier resource-saving trips. Priority is fewer leftover shipments, not cases or tonnes, then cost with bounded preferences.
  • Tie-break default $\varepsilon=\dfrac{0.5}{1+n\,(D+1)}$ with $n$ all built shipments (including past due), $D$ = max_early_days. Weights are computed once before decomposition and reused globally, not recomputed per component. Default total is below 0.5 THB; custom weights must keep $n(Dw_e+w_g)<1$ THB. Exact fractional-baht costs are reported, but sub-baht differences may be outweighed. This is not a cents-resolution guarantee. Upgrade count is the number of LTL members in a shared truck-priced booking, not a truck-size upgrade.
  • Reported separately: total_cost (sum of $a_c$ only), objective_value, tie_break_total, penalty_total.
The MIP gap (how far the solver's answer may be from the proven best answer) must stay 0. The repo default of 0.01 would let a solver accept a plan costing 1% more, which is bigger than every tie-break and would make results arbitrary. The config rejects any gap above 1e-9.

Solving

  • HiGHS (an open-source optimization solver) through SciPy's scipy.optimize.milp, mip_rel_gap = 0 (zero allowed distance from the proven optimum), time limit 600 s (seconds) per component.
  • Decomposition. Shipments that share a candidate are connected. Each connected component is solved separately, typically a province over a short date window. A fleet cap couples the components, so decomposition is skipped when one is set.
  • The partition is re-checked after every solve. A broken cover raises an error.
  • A time-limited incumbent is kept only after integrality, cover and fleet checks. Without a usable incumbent, use known solos without caps or a feasible greedy/all-leftover plan with caps, labelled fallback_*, never optimal. Baseline fleet MILP timeouts also retain status/gap and use only validated covering fleets.
  • Trip status names are optimal, time_limit, infeasible, unbounded, other, prefixed with fallback_ when recovery is used. Recovery has unknown/infinite gap (null in metadata). A capped fallback is feasible but may not maximize coverage.
  • The 600-second setting is per trip-model component, not a total-run budget and not the fleet-pricing limit. Nonoptimal baseline/selected-candidate prices and trip solves emit flags. A zero-gap trip solve proves optimality only for its supplied candidate prices.

How the Excel picks a truck

The EST Truck Usage column on Pivot_Summary is a formula, not a record of what was booked. For each ship-to and day it takes the smallest single truck that fits both limits:

  • 4W if the load is at most 1,500 kg and 4 m³;
  • else 6W if at most 5,000 kg and 25 m³;
  • else 10W if at most 15,000 kg and 35 m³;
  • else 6W × n, where n is the weight divided by 5,000 kg, rounded up. This step ignores volume.

Those are the August capacities. In code, usable types are sorted by kg then m³ capacity, not chosen by rate; an unavailable chosen type can make the sheet comparison unpriced (or use an available LTL case bill). It is deliberately separate from the planner's fleet optimization.

Utilization is the larger of the weight share and the volume share of that truck. The sheet covers only 22 Aug 2025 (57 ship-tos) and never shares a truck between ship-tos. The planner reproduces column EST Truck Usage for all 53 ship-tos that appear in both.

Limits

Approval gates remain open. No business approval is implied by passing tests. Obtain carrier invoices and actual bookings, signed LTL/multi-drop/handling tariffs, customer consolidation and early-arrival permissions, lane transit times, appointment/holiday/dispatch calendars, measured capacities and daily/type/carrier availability. These inputs were not supplied and have not been invented. The master LTL / contract FTL conflict for customer 626378 is retained as a warning while using contract FTL.

  • No route sequencing, so a multi-drop trip is priced, not routed. Drop order and driving time are not modelled.
  • A shipment is never split across dates. A shipment bigger than one truck uses its own solo fleet and cannot share.
  • Shared trips are capped at 3 shipments by default. Raising it grows the candidate list quickly.
  • The savings figure depends on unconfirmed business rules and on a baseline that is computed, not taken from invoices.
  • Floor layout and axle limits are out of scope here. That is loadmax/.
  • Defaults remain unchanged. No business-day transit, appointment/cutoff times, live eligibility/horizon, frozen bookings, holding/force mechanism, hub routing or pallet model.
