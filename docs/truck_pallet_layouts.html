Truck floor maps — three ways to load a 40′ trailer

From Market Configuration LKDC.xlsx (PalletLayout). Each drawing is a different floor grid for the same trailer, not three stacked layers. LoadMax picks one map, then the optimizer only assigns cargo to those numbered pads. The slot MIP (variables, objective, every constraint) is in loadmax_model.html.

What the spreadsheet is telling you

PalletLayout lists several floor sizes (10, 11, 14, 20, 21, 22, 28, 30). The three pictures on the Config sheet are the 40′ options that mix 1.0 m (40″) and 1.2 m (48″) faces of a standard pallet.

The three 40′ floor maps

Door is on the right. Top lane is the left wall (groups A then B). Bottom lane is the right wall (groups C then D). Same 12 m of trailer length in every drawing.

A front-left B rear-left C front-right D rear-right Pad label: position number · mm along trailer length

1. 20-pallet · 40′ · two rows of 10

all same way · 1.2 m along length

Every pallet presents 1.2 m (48″) along the trailer. 10 × 1.2 m = 12 m. Two lanes × 10 = 20 floor spots. Simplest load; fewest pads.

2. 22-pallet · 40′ · one row of 12, one row of 10

left lane turned · +2 spots vs 20

Left lane is rotated: 1.0 m (40″) along the trailer so 12 fit in 12 m (red in the spreadsheet). Right lane stays 1.2 m × 10 (blue). Across: 1.2 m + 1.0 m ≈ trailer width. That is the pinwheel that buys two extra pads.

3. 21-pallet · 40′ · one row of 11, one row of 10

mixed 1.0 m / 1.2 m · between 20 and 22

Same idea as 22, but one fewer turned pallet: 11 + 10 = 21 floor spots. The spreadsheet also lists a shorter 11-pallet / 20′ cut (one row of 6, one of 5) using the same mixed faces.

What HiGHS does with a chosen map

One binary per (container, layer, position). At most one pallet on that pad. Layer 2 stacks on the same position number. Height is summed in that column (A-B: ≤ 9 ft, door pads ≤ 3.5 ft). Compact packing fills from first_plt_n toward both ends using Ass-Seq, not 1, 2, 3 order.

A-B used a denser 30-pallet / 40′ map (two rows of 15), same idea as these three drawings. Switching a market from 20 to 22 is a Config change, not a solver decision. Class caps, when the CSV has them, still use these same pads. The map stays a Config choice.
